Epic vs Ikon vs Indy Pass vs Skiing in Europe: Which Path Is Right for You?

September 1, 2026 · 10 min read

Indy, Epic, Ikon, or an Alps polygon. The maps are the argument.

Four paths: Epic, Ikon, Indy, or just go to Europe and ski a polygon. The workbook tags are the first three. The Alps map is the fourth. Pick the path that matches the days you already take, not the one with the loudest film. The bars are how many atlas ids currently carry each pass tag.

Indy / Epic / Ikon tags in the affiliations file.

Step 1. Indy if the hill is close

Montage is the Indy example already used on this blog. A season of Saturdays at hills like this beats a destination pass you redeem twice. If Drive Time puts an Indy hill inside your usual radius, start there. Upgrade later if you actually fly.

Montage. The close-hill path.

Step 2. Epic / Ikon if you already fly west

Colorado is where those products earn their keep: stacked destination points, long weekend drives from Denver, flights from both coasts. The unlabeled dots are still there. A pass that covers two weeks in Summit County does not cover a season at a Pennsylvania hill. Do not buy both unless you already did both last year.

Colorado. Destination-pass geography.

Step 3. Europe if you want one domain

The 3 Valleys is a week without moving the car. Ski Arlberg is the same idea one country over. An Alps trip is lodging plus a lift pass sold in Europe, not a North American mega-pass with a footnote. If that is the year you have, skip the US pass math.

Alps overview. Domains, not logos.

Step 4. Size still decides the day

Small hills are most of the file. Mega domains are the ads. Match plate size to the trip: carpets and four greens for a first Saturday, a French polygon for a week with strong legs. The pass is a payment method. The map is the choice.

Trail-count plates. Passes sit on a few of these rows.
