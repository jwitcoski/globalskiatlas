<h2>Step 1. Indy if the hill is close</h2>
<p>Montage is the Indy example already used on this blog. A season of Saturdays at hills like this beats a destination pass you redeem twice. If <a href="../DriveTimeMap.html">Drive Time</a> puts an Indy hill inside your usual radius, start there. Upgrade later if you actually fly.</p>

<h2>Step 2. Epic / Ikon if you already fly west</h2>
<p>Colorado is where those products earn their keep: stacked destination points, long weekend drives from Denver, flights from both coasts. The unlabeled dots are still there. A pass that covers two weeks in Summit County does not cover a season at a Pennsylvania hill. Do not buy both unless you already did both last year.</p>

<h2>Step 3. Europe if you want one domain</h2>
<p>The 3 Valleys is a week without moving the car. Ski Arlberg is the same idea one country over. An Alps trip is lodging plus a lift pass sold in Europe, not a North American mega-pass with a footnote. If that is the year you have, skip the US pass math.</p>

<div class="guide-copy">
<h2>Step 4. Size still decides the day</h2>
<p>Small hills are most of the file. Mega domains are the ads. Match plate size to the trip: carpets and four greens for a first Saturday, a French polygon for a week with strong legs. The pass is a payment method. The map is the choice.</p>
