<h2>Step 1. Carpets and chairs, not gondolas</h2>
<p>Magic carpets are the second-most common tagged lift after chairs, once you add platters. First-timers live on carpets and short chairs. Gondolas and cable cars show up later, and they are fewer. If <code>lift_types</code> has carpets, the hill planned for people who still snowplow. If it only has a big aerial, look at the base area before you book a lesson week.</p>

<h2>Step 2. Read the mix</h2>
<p>Akakura Onsen in Niigata, id 937100227, currently shows 23 downhill trails with 17 tagged easy. That is a different beginner day than a hill whose mix is all advanced. The bars are OSM difficulty tags, which mappers sometimes get wrong. Still better than a homepage that calls every run “for the whole family.”</p>

<h2>Step 3. The hill on the map</h2>
<p>Same row on the winter map. Easy ways should reach a lift you can ride. If the greens are a parking-lot ribbon and the rest of the mountain is black, that is a lesson product bolted onto an expert hill. Fine for a destination week with a ski school. Poor for a nervous first Saturday.</p>

<h2>Step 4. Local vs a week away</h2>
<p>Most downhill rows are small. A week in the 3 Valleys is a different trip: you will still ski greens, but you will also walk, ride long lifts, and spend money getting there. For kids, start with the closest mapped hill that has a carpet. Move to mega when the snowplow is gone, not because a ranking said “best for beginners.”</p>
