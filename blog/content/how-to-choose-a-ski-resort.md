## Ten resort websites will give you ten different pitches

One mountain claims "legendary steeps." Another promises "family-friendly fun." Both may be true somewhere on the hill, but neither helps you compare options on the same scale. Global Ski Atlas exists so skiers can evaluate resorts with standardized mapped data — not pass marketing, not influencer hype.

This seven-step framework helps you choose where to ski: skill fit, budget, distance, terrain, crowds, snow, and a final side-by-side comparison of your shortlist. Each step links to tools on this site so you can move from research to decision without opening a dozen tabs.

## Step 1: Match the resort to your skill level

Start honestly. A strong intermediate skier wants different terrain than a first-timer or a double-black chaser.

**Beginners need:**

- Enough green and easy blue terrain (`piste:difficulty=novice` / `easy` in OSM)
- Short lifts back to the learning zone — magic carpets, short chairs, gondolas with mid-mountain bail-outs
- Gentle pitch variety, not one long cat track

**Intermediates need:**

- A large intermediate network — bored intermediates become reckless intermediates
- A few advanced runs to grow into

**Experts need:**

- Steeps, bowls, and trees — `advanced`, `expert`, `freeride` tags where mappers added them
- Lift capacity to those zones without 45-minute traverses

Use [Ski Resort Facts](../SkiResortFacts.html) to filter by trail mix, or read [Best Ski Resorts for Beginners](best-ski-resorts-for-beginners.html) for data-backed learning hills. [Stats explained](ski-resort-stats-explained.html) defines how we count trails and difficulties.

Preview pitch on the [interactive map](../mainmap.html) before you commit. If the map shows nothing green, the brochure photo of a bunny hill may be from another face entirely.

## Step 2: Set a real budget

Lift tickets, lodging, rentals, lessons, and parking add up faster than the pass photo suggests. We do not sell tickets, but we help you avoid paying destination prices for a hill smaller than your local area.

**Budget checklist:**

- **Lift access** — day ticket vs multi-resort pass ([Epic vs Ikon vs Indy vs Europe](epic-ikon-indy-europe-which-to-choose.html) breaks down strategies)
- **Lodging** — on-mountain premium vs valley town shuttle
- **Travel** — flights and rental cars often exceed ticket cost for short trips
- **Food** — base lodge pricing varies wildly by country

Atlas stats do not include prices (they change weekly), but [resort comparison](../resort-comparison.html) helps you ask: "Am I paying Aspen money for a hill half the size of alternatives nearby?" Pair with [drive time map](../DriveTimeMap.html) to find closer options that free budget for an extra day on snow.

## Step 3: Decide how far you will travel

Distance shapes trip length. A two-hour drive supports a day trip; a transcontinental flight demands at least a long weekend to justify jet lag.

**Tools:**

- [drive time map](../DriveTimeMap.html) — isochrone from your home or airport
- [trip planner](../TripPlannerMap.html) — multi-stop routing for road trips
- [multi-resort road trip planner](multi-resort-ski-road-trip-planner.html) — sample itineraries and days-per-stop guidance

If you are visiting the US, [Which U.S. States Have the Most Ski Resorts?](us-states-most-ski-resorts.html) shows where density clusters — useful for combining business travel with a ski day.

Distance also affects gear decisions (rent vs fly with bags) and acclimatization at altitude. Elevation stats in the atlas supplement this step; see [stats explained](ski-resort-stats-explained.html).

## Step 4: Evaluate terrain — not just vertical bragging rights

Vertical drop is one number. Terrain **shape** is the experience.

Ask:

- **Trail count and acreage** — variety vs repeat laps ([largest resorts by mapped terrain](largest-ski-resorts-in-the-world.html))
- **Lift layout** — can you ski top to bottom without lengthy flats?
- **Aspect** — north-facing holds cold snow; south-facing warms early (use [weather map](../weather-map.html) alongside terrain)
- **Tree vs alpine** — glades need mapped pistes or known local knowledge; OSM shows what is drawn

Sort candidates on [resort comparison](../resort-comparison.html) by trails, lifts, and skiable acres. Then verify on the [interactive map](../mainmap.html). Marketing vertical without mapped advanced terrain is a yellow flag.

Advanced skiers: check [Ski Lift Types Explained](ski-lift-types-explained.html) — a resort with only slow two-seaters feels crowded even at moderate trail counts.

## Step 5: Estimate crowds and throughput

Crowds are the hardest stat to standardize. We do not publish live lift-line cameras, but you can proxy congestion:

- **Lift count vs trail count** — few lifts serving many trails means Saturday bottlenecks
- **Resort category** — mega areas absorb crowds; small hills choke on holiday weekends
- **Calendar** — school breaks and pass blackout dates dominate; we do not track these in OSM

Weekday skiing at a medium resort often beats a packed marquee Saturday. If you must travel on peak weeks, favor areas with higher lift-to-trail ratios and multiple base areas.

For pass-holder congestion patterns, [Epic Pass vs Ikon Pass coverage](epic-pass-vs-ikon-pass-resort-coverage.html) shows how many atlas resorts sit on each network — useful if your crowd worry is "everyone with the same pass showed up."

## Step 6: Weigh snow and season length

Snow is weather; weather is local. No atlas stat replaces a forecast three days before travel.

**Use data for structure, forecasts for timing:**

- **Latitude and elevation** from resort facts — higher and colder generally win late season
- **Continental vs maritime** climate — interior Rockies vs Pacific Northwest trade drought risk vs rain risk
- **Historical patterns** — we do not host multi-decade snowfall series in the blog, but elevation and aspect on the [wiki](../wiki/browse.html) help you research

If early-season coverage matters, favor resorts with extensive snowmaking infrastructure — often documented on OSM as `man_made=snow_cannon` near pistes, though coverage is incomplete globally.

Combine [weather map](../weather-map.html) checks with your shortlist the week before departure. Snow bets belong at the end of planning, not the beginning.

## Step 7: Compare your shortlist side by side

You should now have three to five candidates. Put them in [resort comparison](../resort-comparison.html):

| Compare | Why |
| --- | --- |
| Trail count | Variety |
| Skiable acres | Space |
| Lifts | Throughput |
| Vertical | Leg burn / altitude change |
| Trail mix | Skill fit |

Read columns with [stats explained](ski-resort-stats-explained.html) in mind — mapped OSM totals, not brochure peaks.

**Final gut checks:**

- Fly the terrain in the [ski game](/playable/) — experimental, but revealing for pitch and layout
- Read community notes on the [wiki](../wiki/browse.html) if available
- Confirm logistics on [trip planner](../TripPlannerMap.html) one more time

Pick the resort that wins on *your* weighted criteria, not the one with the loudest ad. If two places tie, choose closer or cheaper and ski an extra day.

## Example decision weights

There is no universal formula, but transparent weights beat vibes:

- **Learning trip:** 40% skill fit, 25% budget, 20% distance, 15% snow
- **Powder chase:** 35% snow, 30% terrain, 20% distance, 15% crowds
- **Family vacation:** 30% budget, 25% skill fit, 25% crowds, 20% distance

Adjust percentages, score candidates mentally or in a spreadsheet, and see if the comparison table agrees.

## What we are not optimizing for

Global Ski Atlas does not rank "best" resorts. We surface comparable mapped data so *you* rank them. We are not selling passes, booking hotels, or taking affiliate cuts on lift tickets.

Experimental tools — the [ski game](/playable/), the [wiki](../wiki/browse.html) — help you explore OSM terrain and spot data gaps. If your chosen resort looks thin on the map, consider contributing tags ([how to tag in OSM](how-to-tag-a-ski-resort-in-openstreetmap.html)) so the next skier decides with better information.

## Put it together

1. **Skill** — trail mix and beginner resources
2. **Budget** — ticket, travel, lodging
3. **Distance** — drive time and trip length
4. **Terrain** — trails, acres, vertical, lift layout
5. **Crowds** — lift throughput and calendar
6. **Snow** — elevation, aspect, forecast week-of
7. **Compare** — [resort comparison](../resort-comparison.html) for the decision

Start exploring on the [interactive map](../mainmap.html), narrow with [drive time](../DriveTimeMap.html), and download raw data from [Download Data](../DownloadData.html) if you want to build your own scoring model.

The right resort is the one that matches your ability, wallet, calendar, and appetite for risk — measured honestly, compared fairly.
