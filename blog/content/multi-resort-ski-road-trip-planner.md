One resort is a vacation; three in ten days is a philosophy built on drive time, base-area coffee, and whether the blues match the brochure. Global Ski Atlas built a [Trip Planner](../TripPlannerMap.html) on open resort locations and OSRM driving routes so skiers can chain stops using the same inventory Epic and Ikon never fully map, including independents counted in [which U.S. states have the most ski resorts](us-states-most-ski-resorts.html) and [how many ski resorts worldwide](how-many-ski-resorts-worldwide.html). This essay argues that multi-resort road trips reward open-data routing over pass marketing alone, offers practical days-per-stop guidance, and outlines sample loops in the Rockies, Northeast, and Alps.

Time allocation matters more than mileage bragging. Use one ski day for detours or small hills you want to tick off; two consecutive days to learn lift layout at mountains with thirty or more mapped trails; three or four days at anchor destinations such as large Colorado areas, Utah hubs, or major Alpine bases; and insert a light travel day every fifth or sixth day so you remember fall lines instead of parking lots. A ten-day Rockies loop might open with two days in Summit County from a Frisco base, an indie detour at Loveland, two days at Vail or Beaver Creek, then Wasatch skiing after the drive to Salt Lake—each leg checked in [resort comparison](../resort-comparison.html) and [weather map](../weather-map.html) before lodging is booked. Northeast loops from Boston or Burlington pack Sunapee, Stratton, Mad River Glen, Stowe, and Whiteface into eight days because resorts sit close together, a pattern [drive time map](../DriveTimeMap.html) reveals better than state rankings alone.

The [Trip Planner](../TripPlannerMap.html) turns those ideas into turn-by-turn routes: set a home point or airport hotel, add up to twenty-five resort stops in order, choose whether to end at the last hill or return home, and read OSRM distance and duration before committing. European legs should stay in one continental block because oceans require flights, not road routes; sample Alps itineraries might spend three days in Portes du Soleil or Chamonix, two in Verbier, two in Zermatt with car-free parking at Täsch, and finish in Tyrol or the Dolomites with scale context from [largest ski resorts in the world](largest-ski-resorts-in-the-world.html).
