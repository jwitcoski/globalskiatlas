Global Ski Atlas only works when OpenStreetMap has coherent ski data, and the fastest way to improve the atlas is to tag your local hill correctly in OSM. This essay explains what we need for a downhill resort to appear on the [interactive map](../mainmap.html), how to draw boundaries and map lifts and pistes, and why fixing tags in the community map improves every tool on this site—from the [wiki](../wiki/browse.html) to the [ski game](/playable/) to the downloads on [Download Data](../DownloadData.html).

The foundation of every atlas entry is a `landuse=winter_sports` polygon that outlines the managed ski domain, not the entire mountain range and not just the parking lot. Mappers should add a clear `name` tag and, when helpful, `name:en` for international readers. Without this boundary, our pipeline struggles to group lifts and runs into one resort and to compute fair statistics in [Ski Resort Facts](../SkiResortFacts.html). A common mistake is drawing an oversized polygon that swallows backcountry or neighboring valleys, which inflates skiable acreage and distorts comparisons on [resort comparison](../resort-comparison.html). A tight, accurate boundary is the first requirement for inclusion and for honest stats, as described further in [ski resort stats explained](ski-resort-stats-explained.html).

Lifts and pistes must be tagged on ways that follow real infrastructure and trail centerlines. Lifts use `aerialway=*` values such as `chair_lift`, `gondola`, `t-bar`, and `magic_carpet`, drawn uphill from base to summit with a `name` where known. Downhill runs need `piste:type=downhill` plus `piste:difficulty` values like `easy`, `intermediate`, or `advanced`, with the way direction matching the downhill flow. Tagging pylons as lifts, tracing pistes along lift cables, or leaving summer roads as ski trails are frequent errors that break lift counts on [Ski Lift Facts](../SkiLiftFacts.html) and trail mixes used in [best resorts for beginners](best-ski-resorts-for-beginners.html). Each lift line and piste should be one clean way; duplicates inflate counts until someone merges or deletes the extras.

After tagging, contributors should validate edits by zooming out to confirm the resort reads as a single ski area, spot-checking difficulties relative to one another, and previewing the result in our experimental [ski game](/playable/), which loads the same OSM geometry we ingest. Edits flow through the eleven-step pipeline documented on [Download Data](../DownloadData.html) and appear after the next regional extract—not instantly, but permanently and openly. Remote hills in Eastern Europe, South America, and Central Asia remain the largest opportunities for both count and quality gains discussed in [how many ski resorts worldwide](how-many-ski-resorts-worldwide.html). The **Open in OSM** link in the ski game exists precisely so skiers can close the loop: map, play, fix, and wait for the next atlas build.

Tagging a ski resort in OpenStreetMap is not arcane cartography; it is the practical work that makes a worldwide downhill atlas possible. Draw the winter_sports boundary, map lifts and downhill pistes with specific tags, validate in the game or on the map, and submit your changeset. The atlas improves when mappers improve OSM, and every corrected chairlift or green run helps the next skier compare resorts fairly instead of trusting a brochure alone.
